---
title: Withdraw from a vault
description: Redeem vault shares and route the proceeds to another chain or token.
---

Withdrawing is two independent halves. First you redeem vault shares for the
underlying token on the vault's own chain. Then, optionally, you route that
underlying somewhere else.

The first half always happens. The second is only offered when you ask for a
different chain or token.

## Amounts are denominated in shares

The withdraw field takes **vault shares**, not underlying tokens. A vault whose
share has appreciated is worth more than one underlying per share, so the two
numbers differ.

`WithdrawFlow` reads your share balance on-chain and labels the field
`Amount (in vault shares)`, with the approximate underlying value shown beneath it.
The MAX button and the 25/50/75 shortcuts all operate on the share balance.

<Note>
  If the share-balance read fails, the flow disables submission and shows a retry
  control rather than guessing. Redeeming with the wrong denomination would withdraw
  a different amount than the one displayed.
</Note>

## Redeem on the vault's chain

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enter a share amount">
    Validation compares against your on-chain share balance.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the redeem">
    One transaction. Hexkit waits for the receipt and checks it did not revert.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the balance delta">
    The underlying balance is compared before and after to determine what you
    actually received. That figure sizes any follow-on routing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If the redeem confirms but the balance delta is not yet visible — a stale RPC read
immediately after the receipt — the flow reports the redeem as complete and does not
resubmit it. Your shares are already redeemed at that point; only the routing half
is affected.

## Route the proceeds elsewhere

Choose a different destination chain or token and Hexkit offers one of two routes,
mirroring the deposit side.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Intent route">
    `WithdrawIntentRouteStep` quotes an intent order, escrows the redeemed
    underlying via `open()`, and tracks it on the same four-step timeline —
    **Opened → Signed → Delivered → Settled**.

    As with deposits, the connected account and the fill window are both rechecked
    immediately before signing.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Composer route">
    `withdrawComposerRoute` bridges the redeemed underlying using a Composer route
    and polls for settlement for up to 30 minutes, classifying the result as
    completed, refunded, or partial.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Keeping the underlying instead

Routing is optional at every point. If no route is available, or you would rather
hold the redeemed token on the vault's chain, the flow offers to stop there. The
redeem has already settled, so nothing is lost by declining to route.

## Recovering a stalled withdrawal

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Redeem confirmed, routing did not start | The shares are redeemed. Start a fresh route, or keep the underlying. |
| Route quote expired before signing | Request a new quote. Nothing was escrowed. |
| Order never filled | Refund after `expires`. See [How intent orders work](/concepts/intent-orders). |
| Bridge reported refunded | The underlying returned to the vault's chain. Retry or keep it. |

<Warning>
  As with deposits, do not re-quote a route whose `open()` was already broadcast. A
  fresh quote means a fresh nonce and a second escrow. The retry control is disabled
  while a broadcast transaction is outstanding.
</Warning>
