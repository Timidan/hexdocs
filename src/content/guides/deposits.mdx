The Earn module at `/integrations/lifi-earn` lists yield vaults and deposits into
them. Which path Hexkit takes depends on where your funds are relative to the vault.

## Pick a path

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Same chain">
    Funds and vault on one chain. One approval, one deposit.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Intent route">
    Cross-chain via an escrowed order filled by a solver. The default.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Composer route">
    Cross-chain via a bridge, then a deposit. The fallback.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

`DepositFlow` selects the path for you. When your funding chain differs from the
vault's chain it prefers the intent route and falls back to the composer route when
no intent quote is available.

## Same-chain deposits

Enter an amount and confirm. Hexkit requests a Composer quote, approves the token
for the returned spender if the allowance is short, and submits the deposit.

Approvals go through `safeApproveErc20`, which approves the exact amount rather than
an unlimited allowance and resets a nonzero allowance to zero first when the token
requires it, so USDT-style tokens do not revert.

## Cross-chain deposits over an intent route

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose the funding chain and token">
    The source picker offers the eight chains listed in [Configuration](/configuration).
    Hexkit quotes only routes it can actually build.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the quote">
    `IntentBridgeStep` shows the output you will receive and the deadline the solver
    has to fill it. A quote returning no positive output is rejected rather than
    turned into an order.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approve the input token">
    A standard ERC-20 approval to `INPUT_SETTLER_ESCROW`, for the exact input amount.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the order">
    One `open()` transaction escrows your input. Before signing, Hexkit checks that
    the connected wallet still matches the order and that the fill window is still
    open.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait for delivery">
    The timeline moves **Opened → Signed → Delivered → Settled**. At "Delivered" the
    output is on the destination chain and the vault deposit is offered.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Deposit into the vault">
    Hexkit measures the delivered amount as a balance delta, approves the vault
    spender, and submits the deposit.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If you switch wallet accounts after quoting, the quote resets. The order records the
connected account as both escrow depositor and refund payee, so a stale quote must
not survive an account change.

## Cross-chain deposits over the composer route

When no intent quote is available, `executeCrossChainComposerDeposit` runs a
two-transaction sequence: bridge the token to the destination chain, wait for
settlement, then deposit. Progress appears in the cross-chain timeline.

Settlement polling runs for up to 30 minutes and classifies the outcome as
completed, refunded, or partial. A refunded or partial bridge is reported as such —
it does not continue to the deposit step.

<Note>
  The composer route requires two wallet signatures on two different chains, and the
  app will prompt you to switch networks between them. The intent route requires
  only one signature, on the source chain.
</Note>

## Recovering a stalled deposit

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Quote expired before you opened | Request a new quote. Nothing was signed. |
| `open()` broadcast, receipt slow | Wait. Do not retry — see below. |
| Order never filled | Refund after `expires`. See [How intent orders work](/concepts/intent-orders). |
| Bridge settled, deposit failed | Use the retry control; the delivered amount is re-read. |

<Warning>
  If an `open()` transaction was broadcast, do not request a fresh quote to "try
  again". A retry builds a new order with a new nonce, so both orders can be filled
  and you would bridge twice. Hexkit disables the retry control while a broadcast
  transaction is outstanding and shows its hash instead.
</Warning>

More failure modes are covered in [Troubleshooting](/troubleshooting).
